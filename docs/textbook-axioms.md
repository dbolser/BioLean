## Method

1. For every grounded entity, fetch the OLS4 v2 entity record (`/api/v2/ontologies/{ont}/entities/{iri}`). Use `directAncestor` (is_a closure), `hierarchicalAncestor` (is_a + part_of closure), and `relatedTo` (every existential restriction in subClassOf and equivalentClass, i.e. the logical definition).
2. Subject = first grounded entity that is not a taxon or PATO quality; test it against every other entity, then all other ordered pairs (flagged "non-default pair order").
3. Relation tests (claim relation -> axiom accepted):
   - is_a: object in is_a closure, or CHEBI `has_role` object (e.g. iron cation has_role cofactor).
   - part_of / located_in: object in hierarchicalAncestors but *not* reached by is_a alone; or `part_of` / `located_in` / `occurs_in` restriction; or inverse `has_part`.
   - has_part / made_of / contains: inverse part_of as above, or `has_part` restriction on the subject or any is_a ancestor.
   - occurs_in, has_input (incl. has_primary_input), has_output (incl. has_primary_output), capable_of (incl. capable_of_part_of): matching restriction on the subject or an is_a ancestor; the filler may be the object or a subclass of it.
   - CHEBI acid/base conjugates and tautomers count as the same chemical (GO uses pyruvate(1-), textbooks say pyruvic acid).
4. Ungrounded target: if an is_a / part_of ancestor of the subject is named verbatim in the claim but was never grounded as an entity, it counts as an axiom and is flagged (3 claims, e.g. "...are organ systems").
5. Definition text: one entity's IAO:0000115 definition (or rdfs:comment) names another entity (label, synonym, or source text), or for one-entity claims covers at least half the claim's content words. Label/synonym hits for "also called" claims go here too (synonyms are annotations, not axioms).
6. Manual review: I read every definition_text_only hit. 19 where the definition only *names* the other term without stating the claim were moved to not_in_ontology. 2 axiom hits were dropped: an FtsZ/GTP match that came only from "GTP binding has_input GTP", and "salivary gland is_a organ" for an "accessory organ" claim.

## Discussion

- **Only about 15% of these claims are already axioms.** Nearly all of them are anatomy partonomy and is_a in UBERON (heart has atria, brain has thalamus, adrenal gland has cortex/medulla, thyroid is_a endocrine gland) plus a few GO cellular-component part_of links (stroma part_of chloroplast, nuclear pore part_of nuclear envelope). The body-systems-b file alone has 15 of the 36.
- **The textbook mostly says things only definitions say.** About 26% are stated in the definition sentence but not as logic: "photosynthesis uses CO2", "Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH", "translation uses mRNA", "liver stores glycogen", "TCA cycle enzymes are in the mitochondrial matrix". These are the cheapest wins for BioLean: definition prose that could become formal axioms.
- **Process-level facts are the big gap.** GO `photosynthesis` (GO:0015979) has *no* logical axioms beyond is_a metabolic process: no has_input CO2/water, no has_output glucose/O2, no occurs_in chloroplast. Same for glycolysis occurs_in cytoplasm, transcription occurs_in nucleus, and Calvin cycle occurs_in stroma. GO stays generic on purpose (it is cross-species), so the location facts textbooks teach as defaults are left out. Glycolysis is the exception: it has has_primary_output pyruvate and part_of aerobic respiration.
- **Many claims cannot be axioms in these four ontologies anyway.** Taxon-level claims ("plants are capable of cellular respiration", "bacteria have no nucleus"), quality and count claims (double-stranded, four chambers, haploid), negation (`lacks_part`), and relations GO/UBERON don't use (connects, attached_to, permeable_to). About 44 of the 142 misses have fewer than 2 grounded entities, or none from GO/UBERON/CL/CHEBI.
- **Grounding noise hides some axioms.** Where the class term was never grounded (e.g. "muscle tissue", "organ system"), the axiom exists but the claim's entity list misses it. A rescue pass (step 4) caught 3 of these. More are likely, so 15% is a lower bound.
- **Caveats.** The definition-text check is a lexical heuristic plus a manual pass, not a careful judgement on every claim. Borderline items (e.g. "integral proteins are called transport proteins", matched via channel activity is_a transporter activity) are kept as hits. One definition hit is on an obsolete term (GO:0005844 polysome).
